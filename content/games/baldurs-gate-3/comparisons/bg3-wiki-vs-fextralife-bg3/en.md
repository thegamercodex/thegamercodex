Almost any Baldur's Gate 3 question ends up at one of two wikis: bg3.wiki or the Fextralife wiki. Both cover the whole game, but with different editorial philosophies. bg3.wiki is a database that prioritizes precision; Fextralife is a broad guide that prioritizes accompanying you. Knowing which to open for a given question saves time.

## Precision vs orientation

bg3.wiki is a community effort focused on exact data. More than 13,000 pages documenting rules, items, spells, and mechanics rigorously, including the differences between the game and D&D 5e tabletop rules. When you need to confirm a number, the precise wording of an ability, or how a modifier actually stacks, it's the source the community considers most reliable. The cost is that it prioritizes precision over friendliness: for an absolute beginner it can feel dry.

Fextralife goes for orientation. Walkthroughs by act, companion and romance guides, build breakdowns, and an interactive map, all in a narrated, accessible format. Rather than only recording data, it walks you through the game. It's ideal for a first playthrough, where what's missing isn't a single data point but a guide that orders the chaos. In exchange, on fine mechanics detail it's less exact than bg3.wiki, and its experience carries plenty of ads.

## Where each one shines

The difference shows clearly by question type. "How much damage does this item do exactly and how does it scale?" is bg3.wiki territory: precise, verified data. "How do I advance this quest without breaking it and how do I raise this companion's approval?" is Fextralife territory: step-by-step guidance that accompanies you.

The interactive map is another Fextralife card. bg3.wiki is text and data, with no navigable map; if you want to locate something visually, Fextralife — or a dedicated map tool like Map Genie — handles that task better.

## The combination the community uses

In practice, many players don't pick one: they use both. Fextralife to orient — advancing quests, managing companions, deciding a build — and bg3.wiki to pin things down — confirming the exact number when the build depends on it. One tells you where to go; the other confirms the detail.

## When each one wins

| Case | Better option |
|---|---|
| Verifying an exact mechanic or interaction | bg3.wiki |
| Advancing a quest step by step | Fextralife |
| A spell's wording or an item's scaling | bg3.wiki |
| Romances and companion approval | Fextralife |
| Interactive map to orient yourself | Fextralife |

## Recommendation

If your question is about data — numbers, rules, interactions — open bg3.wiki. If your question is about guidance — how to progress, who to recruit, how to romance a companion — open Fextralife. For a first playthrough, Fextralife as your go-to guide and bg3.wiki as a second screen to verify; for serious theorycrafting, bg3.wiki up front. They aren't rivals to choose between, but two complementary layers. Each tool has its full analysis in the codex.
