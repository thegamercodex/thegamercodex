Final Fantasy XIV's endgame —savage and ultimates— demands a precision the game doesn't teach you directly. You need to know your rotation in detail, know which gear to chase, and analyze why your pull failed. That whole layer lives outside the client, in community tools most serious raiders keep on hand. This stack gathers the six essentials.

## A raider's workflow

The theory base is **The Balance**: the community source of job guides, with rotations, openers, and priorities maintained by theorycrafters. It's where you learn to play your job correctly.

With the rotation clear, **Etro** lets you plan your target gear set and calculate stats before farming anything, so you know exactly which pieces to chase and how to meld. After each pull, **FF Logs** turns your fight into data: percentile parse, uptime, and rankings against the rest of the world. And **XIVAnalysis** complements it with automated analysis flagging concrete mistakes —a dropped cooldown, a rotation drift.

Two more tools handle daily life: **Teamcraft** covers crafting and gathering —material lists and routes— to supply your own consumables, and **XIVLauncher** is the alternative launcher with quality-of-life plugins that speed up login and a thousand small frictions.

## Why these six

Each covers a part of endgame without redundancy:

- **Theory and rotation** → The Balance
- **Gear planning** → Etro
- **Performance analysis** → FF Logs + XIVAnalysis
- **Crafting and consumables** → Teamcraft
- **Quality of life** → XIVLauncher

You don't need all six for normal content, but for high-difficulty raids they're the standard set. Each tool has its full analysis in the codex.
