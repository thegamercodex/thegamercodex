Both are references, but they serve different moments of the game. Kiranico is the primary data source: the exact number behind everything. Game8 is the wiki that wraps that data in readable guides. When a guide says "this monster is weak to fire," the exact figure lives on Kiranico; the explanation of what to do with it lives on Game8.

## Kiranico: the source of truth

Kiranico datamines the game and exposes raw data: hitzone values per part and damage type, drop rates per method, motion values, materials, weapon trees. It's what other tools and guides cite as their source. For serious theorycrafting, where "roughly weak to fire" isn't enough and you need the exact number, Kiranico is indispensable.

## Game8: the friendly guide

Game8 takes that data and turns it into answers. Per-weapon meta builds, readable weakness tables, a walkthrough, an armor set search. It's optimized to answer a player's concrete question with a clear article. It's the first search reflex for almost any Wilds question.

## How the use case decides

**Exact hitzone?** Kiranico. The datamined number.

**A written answer to a question?** Game8. Clear article.

**Precise drop rates?** Kiranico. Percentage per method.

**A ready per-weapon build?** Game8. With its explanation.

**Motion values for a calculator?** Kiranico. Primary source.

## Limitations

**Kiranico**: raw data without context, reading curve, depends on the datamine for new content.

**Game8**: ads and dense layout, variable technical depth, the Japanese translation sometimes trails by a step.

## Verdict

They don't compete: **Kiranico** is the exact-data source, **Game8** is the guide that explains it. The newcomer starts on Game8; the optimizer drops to Kiranico when they need the precise number. The ideal is using Game8 to understand and Kiranico to verify the hard data.
