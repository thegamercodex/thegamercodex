---
title: "WildsBuilder vs Rayleon — one solution or every solution"
description: "Two independent MH Wilds set builders with opposite philosophies: WildsBuilder gives you a clean set fast, Rayleon shows you the full space of combinations."
---

Both solve the same puzzle—which armor and decorations meet your skills—but answer differently. WildsBuilder hands you a polished, ready-to-share solution. Rayleon hands you every solution so you decide. The choice depends on whether you want speed or total control.

## WildsBuilder: one clean solution

WildsBuilder is built for speed: you mark skills, get a set, share it with a link. It doesn't overwhelm you with alternatives; it gives a direct answer that works. For most players, that's exactly what they need: a set that meets the skills without comparing twenty variants.

## Rayleon: the full space

Rayleon is an exhaustive solver. Instead of one solution, it enumerates every combination of armor and decorations that meets your skills, sorted by criteria like free slots. For the optimizer who wants to pick the combination with the most room for extra skills or a specific resistance, seeing the whole space is the difference.

## How the use case decides

**Want a set fast?** WildsBuilder. A direct answer, no noise.

**Want to see every option?** Rayleon. It enumerates the full space.

**Maximizing free slots?** Rayleon. It sorts solutions so you pick the best.

**Sharing the build?** WildsBuilder. Link sharing is its strength.

**Hunting a hard edge case?** Rayleon. Exhaustive search confirms whether it exists.

## Limitations

**WildsBuilder**: it doesn't expose the full solution space, so for fine optimization it falls short. No damage calculation.

**Rayleon**: the volume of results can overwhelm a casual player. No link sharing, no damage, austere interface.

## Verdict

**WildsBuilder** for most: speed and sharing. **Rayleon** for the optimizer who wants control over every combination. Many use WildsBuilder daily and turn to Rayleon when a hard set demands seeing every option. They don't compete so much as cover two different levels of demand.
