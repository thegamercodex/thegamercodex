Monster Hunter Wilds hides almost all of its math: it won't tell you hitzones, motion values, or whether one set outperforms another. Truly optimizing means leaving the game for a set of tools that cover each layer of the process, from deciding what to build to confirming in real time that it works. This six-tool stack covers that cycle end to end.

## The optimization flow

It all starts with the decision. **Maxroll MH Wilds** is the core: explained per-weapon meta builds with farming routes. It tells you what to build and why. With that idea clear, **WildsBuilder** solves the concrete set—which armor and decorations meet the skills—and lets you share it with a link.

Before investing hours farming, it's worth verifying. **MH Wilds Calculator**, open-source, models the damage formula to confirm with numbers whether your set performs as expected. And when a number doesn't add up, **Kiranico** is the source of truth: exact datamined hitzones, drop rates, and motion values that feed both the build and the calculator.

With the set decided, it's time to get it. **AtlasForge** locates on its interactive map where each material, monster, and endemic life is, so the farming session is efficient. And in the hunt itself, **HunterPie** shows monster HP and your damage in real time over the PC client, closing the loop: you confirm live that the plan works.

## Why these six

Each covers a distinct layer of the process:

- **Decision and guides** → Maxroll MH Wilds
- **Set assembly** → WildsBuilder
- **Damage verification** → MH Wilds Calculator
- **Raw data** → Kiranico
- **Farming logistics** → AtlasForge
- **Live feedback** → HunterPie

You don't need all six to hunt casually, but together they're what separates informed optimization from trial and error. Each tool has its full analysis in the codex.
