title: poe.ninja vs PoE 2 Scout — which economy tracker to use for PoE 2
description: Short comparison between two price trackers and market data tools for Path of Exile 2.
---

Knowing what things are worth in PoE 2 is necessary as soon as you start trading. Two web tools cover that space: **poe.ninja**, the consolidated standard from PoE 1 with coverage already extended to PoE 2, and **PoE 2 Scout**, a PoE 2-native alternative built open source.

## Key difference

**poe.ninja** is the community reference people have known since PoE 1. Its tracking infrastructure has years of real use across thousands of players, which means the historical charts, trends, and category coverage are already consolidated. PoE 2 is just another section within the same platform. If you used poe.ninja in PoE 1, you feel at home in PoE 2.

**PoE 2 Scout** was built native for PoE 2, focused specifically on the new game's market specifics (orbs with unique behaviors, different currency hierarchies, items without PoE 1 equivalents). It's open source under MIT and the code lives on GitHub, which matters to some users when trusting a tool with their data.

## When to use each one

- **If you want consolidated, mature market tracking** → poe.ninja. Proven infrastructure.
- **If the build browser for PoE 2 ladder meta interests you** → poe.ninja. PoE 2 Scout doesn't cover build meta-data.
- **If you prefer auditable open-source code** → PoE 2 Scout. MIT on GitHub.
- **If you want a tool scoped only to PoE 2 with no PoE 1 mixing** → PoE 2 Scout. UI without PoE 1 legacy.

## Considerations

Both are web-based with no login and no individual user tracking. They aggregate data via public trade scraping, so their numbers should converge in liquid markets.

Most reasonable path for most players: **poe.ninja** by default — it's what most of the community uses and references. **PoE 2 Scout** is worth a second opinion, especially when you're evaluating a PoE 2-specific item where the native model can catch nuances poe.ninja's inherited system models in less detail.

As with all these economy tool comparisons, **opening both in parallel tabs** during an expensive decision costs zero and gives extra signal.
