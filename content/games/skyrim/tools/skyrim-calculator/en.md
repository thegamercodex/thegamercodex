## What it is

Skyrim Calculator is a free web tool hosted at `skyrimcalculator.com`. Part of the "Elder Scrolls calculators" ecosystem, independently maintained, covering Skyrim Special/Anniversary Edition (including DLC Dawnguard, Hearthfire, and Dragonborn).

Functionally: it lets you select perks from each skill tree visually, configure your attributes (Health/Magicka/Stamina) for a target level, and see the estimated final stats. Supports shareable URLs to save your build.

## What problem it solves

Skyrim has 18 skill trees and ~250 perks. When you start a playthrough, you vaguely know what class you want (mage, stealth, two-handed), but:
- How many perks will you have at level 50 / 70 / 100?
- Which specific perks pay off most in early vs. late game?
- How do you combine 2-3 skill trees without overspending?

Without a planner, you end up spending perks reactively, then at level 40 you discover you don't have enough stamina or that a critical Conjuration perk requires another one you forgot.

Skyrim Calculator solves that by enabling visual planning before you commit.

## Differentiation

Versus **manual planning with UESP**: UESP has the complete data but in wiki format. It doesn't simulate constraints, doesn't preview the full build. Skyrim Calculator is interactive.

Versus **in-game respec mods**: respec mods exist (Path of Pure Souls, etc.) but require modding. Skyrim Calculator is pre-play planning and also works for vanilla playthroughs on console.

Versus **homemade Excel sheets**: people build their own spreadsheets. The Calculator does the same with native UI and pre-loaded data.

## What people use it for

**Planning a first playthrough**: new players decide "I'll go mage" but don't know which perks to prioritize. The Calculator shows them which tree to take first.

**Hybrid builds (multi-class)**: for builds like "stealth archer + light armor + alchemy", knowing how many perks you have to spare is critical. The Calculator sums points automatically.

**Endgame min-maxing**: players aiming for level 60+ can see exactly what it takes to reach endgame perks (e.g., Slow Time, Master Conjuration).

**Sharing builds**: the exportable URL lets you send your build to a friend or post on Reddit/Discord.

**Visual reference for skill trees**: even when not planning, navigating the trees in the UI is faster than opening UESP for each perk.

## Who this tool is NOT for

If your modlist includes Ordinator, Vokrii, SkyRem, or any perk overhaul, Skyrim Calculator doesn't apply — the perks differ. For Ordinator there are specific calculator-mods.

If you play Requiem (massive balance overhaul), Skyrim Calculator doesn't reflect Requiem's economy.

If you like improvising and don't plan builds, the Calculator is overhead.

## How to use it in practice

1. Open `skyrimcalculator.com` in any browser.
2. First time, it shows an overview of all skill trees.
3. Click each tree to see its perks. Check the ones you want to take.
4. The top counter shows how many perk points you've spent total.
5. To configure attributes: there's a separate panel for distributing Health/Magicka/Stamina per level-up.
6. When done, copy the URL — it encodes your complete build. Share or save.

## Honest limitations

**Vanilla-only**. Doesn't support perk overhauls like Ordinator. If you mod perks, the Calculator doesn't apply.

**Doesn't simulate real combat**. It tells you what perks to take, not whether your build will be viable against Master-difficulty dragons. That requires real experience.

**UI isn't the most modern**. Functional but austere. Mobile experience is limited.

**Not actively updated**. The tool hasn't added major features recently. It covers vanilla well, don't expect new functionality.

**Ad-supported**. The page has banner ads. AdBlock works but the author relies on traffic.

## How to get started

1. Open `skyrimcalculator.com`.
2. Decide your playstyle: mage, warrior, thief, hybrid.
3. Check target perks in each relevant tree.
4. Verify your total spent perks is reasonable for the level you plan to reach.
5. Configure attributes as needed.
6. Copy the URL and save.

For vanilla Skyrim, it's the go-to. If you mod perks, look for calculators specific to the mod (e.g., Ordinator has its own fan-made planner).
