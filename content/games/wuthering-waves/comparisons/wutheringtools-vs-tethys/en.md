In Wuthering Waves, "how much damage do I do?" is a deceptively simple question. Between the main stat and substats across five Echoes, the Sonata effects, the equipped weapon, the Resonance Chain (S0 to S6) and rotation buffs, a Resonator's real damage is the sum of many interacting variables. There are two ways to attack that. WutheringTools chooses to give you an actionable answer fast: plug in your build, see your number, know what to touch first. Tethys chooses to give you the full laboratory: compare options granularly, move variables and understand the why behind every change. Both calculate damage, but they answer different questions and demand very different levels of commitment.

## Philosophy: answer vs laboratory

WutheringTools is built around a practical question: given this Resonator with this build, how much do I hit and which piece do I change to hit more? It's an action-oriented damage calculator. You feed in your stats, out comes a DPS output for that character, and —most useful— it points you toward which upgrade pays off most next: leveling an Echo, improving a substat, swapping a weapon. The learning curve is deliberately low. It doesn't ask you to understand the whole damage model before getting value; it asks for your build and hands back something actionable. For the player who farms Echoes all week and just wants to know where to spend the next Waveplate, that's exactly the right level of detail.

Tethys stands at the other end. It's a theorycraft tool built to compare in depth, not just report a number. Where WutheringTools gives you "your build does X," Tethys lets you ask "what happens if I swap this weapon for that one, go from S0 to S1 and adjust this substat spread?" and see the exact delta of each decision. That power comes with a curve: more inputs, more visible concepts and more responsibility on the user to configure the scenario correctly. It's not the tool for a thirty-second answer; it's the tool for an afternoon of deciding, with data, what's worth pulling or investing in.

## Granular comparisons: weapons, Sequences and substats

This is where the difference becomes concrete. The three most expensive decisions in Wuthering Waves —which weapon to pull, whether to raise the Resonance Chain, and how to spread your Echo substats— are exactly the terrain where Tethys shines and WutheringTools, by design, falls short.

**Weapons.** Each Resonator has a weapon type (Broadblade, Sword, Pistol, Gauntlet, Rectifier) and within that type several options with very different passives. Deciding whether to pull a banner weapon or stick with a free-to-play alternative is one of the most serious bets for an F2P player. WutheringTools tells you how much damage your build does with the weapon you already have; Tethys lets you put two weapons side by side, with their passives and scalings, and see which wins in your specific setup and by how much. That direct comparison is what turns a hunch into an informed decision.

**Sequences (Resonance Chain).** Going from S0 to S1, or S5 to S6, costs convenes and sometimes decides whether you pull one more copy of a character instead of a new one. The value of each Sequence varies enormously between Resonators: some jumps are huge, others marginal. WutheringTools isn't built to answer "is this Sequence worth the investment?" precisely; Tethys is, because it lets you model the build at S0 and at S1 and read the real damage delta between them. For an F2P player counting every Astrite, that delta is the difference between a good pull and a regret.

**Substats.** Echo substats are controlled noise: crit rate, crit damage, ATK%, elemental damage, etc., and the recurring question is which of them adds the most marginal damage in your current build. WutheringTools points you toward the next upgrade in general terms; Tethys lets you tune the spread substat by substat and see which change moves the needle most, which matters once you're in the endgame polishing pieces for Tower of Adversity and every point of crit counts.

## Learning curve and who each one serves

It's worth being honest about the cost of entry. WutheringTools wins on accessibility: if you've never used a damage calc, you can get value from it in minutes. Tethys asks for more —understanding which inputs to configure, what they mean, and accepting that the tool doesn't decide for you, it just shows you the numbers so you can decide. That curve is a feature, not a bug: the depth that makes it useful for comparing in detail is the same thing that makes it slower for a quick answer.

That's why they don't really compete for the same user at the same moment. WutheringTools is for the average player who wants to optimize without becoming a theorycrafter. Tethys is for the theorycrafter —or the player willing to act as one for an afternoon— who needs certainty before spending limited resources.

## When each one wins

| Case | Winner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Knowing how much damage my current build does and which piece to change first | WutheringTools | Direct DPS output + actionable guidance on the next upgrade |
| Comparing two weapons in depth to decide which to pull | Tethys | Side-by-side comparison of passives and scalings in your specific setup |
| Evaluating whether going from S0 to S1 is worth the investment | Tethys | Models the build at both Sequences and shows the real damage delta |
| Quick, actionable optimization without learning a new tool | WutheringTools | Low learning curve, value in minutes |
| Prioritizing which substat adds the most marginal damage | Tethys | Lets you tune the spread substat by substat and read each delta |

The underlying trade-off is depth against friction. WutheringTools sacrifices granularity to give you a useful answer without having to learn anything new; Tethys sacrifices immediacy to give you the full control that comparing weapons, Sequences and substats with rigor demands. The choice isn't which is "better," but which question you're facing: if it's "what do I change now?", open WutheringTools; if it's "what's worth pulling or raising, and by how much?", go to Tethys and take your time.
